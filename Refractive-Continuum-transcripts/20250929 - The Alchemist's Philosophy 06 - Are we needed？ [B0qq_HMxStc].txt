Perfection by its very definition has no need or lack. A truly self-sufficient being cannot be deficient in any way. Then why would perfection create an imperfect world, a world of voids that invites participation through incompleteness. The key to this puzzle lies in the concept of distinctness. To allow for a genuine relationship, a form of separation is necessary. Our sense of self is an echo of this principle built from layers of unity that are arise from separation on every level of the physical and cognitive scale. Distinction creates a space where parts can form a relationship with each other.

We are a symphony of relationships between the cells and systems that compose us. This primary division between universal oneness and our created unity is what allows for a relationship with the divine. It is not perfection that is in need but the process of unification. The drawing together of the many towards the one that calls for our active participation. This is the nature of the relationship. Similarly, we can only relate to anything, another person, the world or even our own self because of this fundamental distinction. Our consciousness is a set of unified cognitive structures and it relates to itself in two ways.

Externally through whatever is not self and internally in relation to the parts that embody it. The very distinction between mind and body are two levels of this separation. Life is cognitive and our embodiment is unified in function. A vessel of both physical mechanics and cognitive structures. From these layers of unity, the illusion of a separate self resolves into consciousness. This creates a cognitive world, a world within a world that operates seemingly independent from reality. yet is wholly dependent upon it. In physics, space, time, and thus energy itself are born from the relationship between parts.

True unity is not the absence of parts, but the harmonious relationships among them. The parts themselves are but the dust of the earth. It is the relationship that breathes life into them. Thus, our soul is literally our relationship with the divine. Perfection itself has no need of us but our relationship with it does. Living requires effort. It is our effort that reveals the need in the world. This need is not a flaw in the creator but a role for the created. It is a divine invitation to be partners where our relationship with creation is the arena where that partnership is forged.

Thus to tie it back to the original question, are we needed? The answer is yes. Not by perfection itself, but by our own imperfection, by the effort we invest and by the relationships we forge within ourselves, our families, our communities, and with the perfect oneness that all things strive to unify towards. It is thus the relationship with oneness where we are all ultimately needed.
