\section*{1. Kinematic Parametrization (Clebsch Variables)}
A continuous metric governed by a scalar potential ($\nabla h$) is irrotational ($\nabla \times \nabla h = 0$).  Topological defects and rotational shear are formalized by parameterizing the macroscopic velocity field $\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{x}, t)$ using Clebsch variables \cite{Clebsch1859}. 

Spatial vorticity is formally captured by defining the continuous velocity field via orthogonal conjugate components:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{v}(\mathbf{x}, t) = \nabla h + \mathbf{v}_{\perp} + \lambda_\Omega\nabla\beta_\Omega \label{eq:tensor_velocity_field} 
\end{equation}
Where $\nabla h$ represents the irrotational longitudinal phase (compression and translation), $\mathbf{v}_{\perp}$ represents propagating transverse shear, and $\lambda_\Omega\nabla\beta_\Omega$ represents the internal rotational phase (topological twisting). The spatial gradient operator ($\nabla$) is applied with respect to the flat Euclidean background metric ($\delta_{ij}$ in $\mathbb{R}^3$). 

Taking the spatial curl of the rotational velocity field extracts the internal topological vorticity ($\boldsymbol{\Omega}$):
\begin{align}
    \boldsymbol{\Omega} &= \nabla \times (\lambda_\Omega\nabla\beta_\Omega) \nonumber \\ 
    \boldsymbol{\Omega} &= \nabla\lambda_\Omega \times \nabla\beta_\Omega \label{eq:tensor_vorticity} 
\end{align}
This parametrization dictates the kinematic behavior of the metric: the irrotational phase generates longitudinal dynamic pressure, while the Clebsch rotational phase generates spatial vorticity and the resulting distortional shear stress required for invariant rest mass \cite{Volovik2003}.

\section*{2. The Thermodynamic Budget (Bernoulli Conservation)}
Integrating the steady-flow Euler equation for an incompressible fluid ($\int \frac{dP}{\rho} + \mathbf{v} \cdot d\mathbf{v} = 0$) along a streamline to the phase limit ($c$) defines the finite yield stress of the continuous metric. This maximum structural limit establishes the Critical Pressure ($P_c = \frac{1}{2}\rho_\tau c^2$). By the conservation of energy (Bernoulli's Principle) \cite{Landau1987}, the sum of the local static pressure ($P_{static}$) and all applied kinematic loads equates to this unperturbed limit.

A localized coordinate grid is subjected to two scales of structural load:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Macroscopic Load ($\Delta P_{macro}$):} The depletion of ambient capacity induced by the background cosmic expansion and localized gravitational bodies. Integrating the macroscopic Hubble parameter ($v = H_0 r$) to the causal boundary determines the continuous macroscopic velocity field of the expanding metric ($v_0(r) = c(1 - e^{-r/R_h})$). This background dynamic pressure yields $\Delta P_{macro} = \frac{1}{2}\rho_\tau v_0^2$, establishing a background equation of state dictating maximum metric compliance at the coordinate origin ($r=0, v_0=0$) and minimum compliance at the causal horizon ($v_0 \to c$).
    \item \textbf{Localized Microscopic Load ($P_{dyn} + P_{shear}$):} The kinetic translation and structural vorticity of a localized topological boundary.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{The Nested Ledger:}\\
The ambient capacity of a localized frame pre-strained by this macroscopic background establishes the Ambient Pressure ($P_{ambient}$):
\begin{equation}
    P_{ambient} = P_c - \Delta P_{macro} = P_c - \frac{1}{2}\rho_\tau v_0^2
\end{equation}
This macroscopic pressure deficit translates into the fractional Gravitational Compliance ($\alpha_g$):
\begin{equation}
    \alpha_g^2 = \frac{P_{ambient}}{P_c} = 1 - \frac{v_0^2}{c^2} \implies P_{ambient} = P_c \alpha_g^2
\end{equation}

In line with the Bernoulli Principle, localized kinematics consume this available ambient capacity. Dynamic pressure ($P_{dyn}$) is generated by the root-sum-square of all orthogonal velocities, scaled against the background density ($\rho_\tau$):
\begin{equation}
    P_{dyn} = \frac{1}{2} \rho_\tau \left( v_\parallel^2 + v_\perp^2 + v_\circlearrowleft^2 \right) \label{eq:tensor_pdyn}
\end{equation}
The distortional shear stress ($P_{shear}$) is governed by the structural magnitude of the localized Clebsch vorticity ($\boldsymbol{\Omega}$). 

Subtracting this localized load determines the remaining static pressure ($P_{static}$), which acts as the structural ledger of the continuous geometry:
\begin{equation}
    P_{static} = P_{ambient} - (P_{dyn} + P_{shear}) \label{eq:tensor_pstatic}
\end{equation}
The Ambient Kinematic Compliance ($\alpha_a$) quantifies the capacity consumed by localized mechanics:
\begin{equation}
    \alpha_a^2 = \frac{P_{static}}{P_{ambient}}
\end{equation}
The dimensionless Total Scalar Admittance ($\alpha_s^2$) of the localized coordinate resolves to the product of the nested compliances:
\begin{equation}
    \alpha_s^2 \equiv \frac{P_{static}}{P_c} = \left( \frac{P_{ambient}}{P_c} \right) \left( \frac{P_{static}}{P_{ambient}} \right) = \alpha_g^2 \alpha_a^2 \label{eq:tensor_admittance}
\end{equation}

    \item $\alpha$: \textbf{Viscoelastic Modulus Ratio.} The dimensionless transverse coupling constant. It tracks the fractional capacity of the metric to sustain transverse shear relative to bulk compression.

Defining the localized dynamic density as $\rho_{dyn} = \rho_\tau v^2/c^2$, the tensor outputs the macroscopic Eulerian momentum density flux ($\hat{T}^{0i} = c \rho_{dyn} v^i$). \subsection*{5.1 The Equation of Motion (Navier-Stokes)}
