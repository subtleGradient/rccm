03 · Spend

Extra motion consumes static pressure capacity. The gap grows amber as q falls; its spatial matrix weights rise.

04 · Compare

The surrounding higher-pressure fluid pushes toward the depleted region. Reversing one circulation changes the overlap and reverses the pressure imbalance.

q = 1 − |u₁ + u₂|²
|u₁ + u₂|² = |u₁|² + |u₂|² + 2u₁ · u₂

Here u = v⊥ / c. The overlap term is signed. Opposing flows give a negative cross term and restore capacity relative to the separate loads.

a = −∇Pstatic / ρτ
F = −Veff ∇Pstatic

For the mass scene, the amber regions are prescribed pressure wells. More distant fluid retains more pressure. The displayed force follows the pressure slope through each object.

Predict: switch positive + positive to negative + negative. What changes?

Every circulation and slip arrow reverses. Squared speed, q, reciprocal packing and the repulsive pressure resultant stay the same. The matrix’s antisymmetric entries change sign while its diagonal entries stay fixed.

This is a frozen, smooth phase-overlap specimen. Each core at x = ±1.5 supplies uᵢ = sᵢ a(−y, x−xᵢ, 0) exp(−rᵢ² / 2σ²), with σ = 1.25 and each isolated peak speed 0.38c. This divergence-free field makes the sign experiment reproducible. We assemble e = u₁ + u₂ and b = 0.2 curl(u₁ + u₂), holding α = 1 and tₚ = 0.2 in scene units. For this specimen, the slip and rotational diagnostic use the same prescribed circulation field. Section 1 keeps those channels independently parameterized; this is a chosen coupling for the picture. Other ledger loads, including P_shear, are zero. The mass scene instead uses two Gaussian deficits of amplitude 0.28 and width 1.25; the uniform scene holds q = 0.72.
