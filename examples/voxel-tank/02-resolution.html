Inside this specimen

A field first. A picture second.

The electron / positron pair supplies the scene. An authored toroidal Gaussian g supplies the frozen geometry: capacity drops through a doughnut-shaped band; azimuthal slip circulates around it; the curl of a separate rotational field supplies twist. Reversing handedness reverses e and b while preserving q and RMS activity.

The fixture uses c = α = tp = 1 and Pshear = 0 in normalized units. Its chosen background load is L = 0.65g. Slip and rotation spend the remaining pressure budget: q = 1 − L − |e|² − |vrot|². The broad rotational envelope contributes to that budget everywhere it turns.

These are two independently sampled reference defects: each box contains its own complete authored field. Their signs can match or oppose. The between study combines overlapping fields; this study compares observation windows.

The fine field lives at fixed positions. Each displayed cell receives a sampled volume average over its own subvolume. At 64 cells per defect, each visible cell still contains 512 sample points.

Concepts: GfX §1 (slip and Clebsch rotation), §3.1–3.3 (q, e, b), §8 (mass as a rotational boundary), §9.2 (electric / magnetic cross-coupling). This study chooses the specimen’s geometry; the controls operate its observation window.

Predict: opposed pair, one voxel each. Does zero pair-mean twist mean quiet fluid?

No. The two signed orientations cancel in the pair average. RMS twist stays positive. This is the distinction in GfX §4.3: the averaged antisymmetric channel can vanish while microscopic activity remains present. Flip to aligned circulation and watch the mean return without changing the activity.

The nonlinear averaging cut

Average the matrix you actually have.

The temporal diagonal contains −q; the spatial diagonals contain 1/q. So the coarse cell must carry ⟨q⟩ and ⟨1/q⟩ separately. A mean capacity alone cannot recreate its spatial weight.

A low-capacity pocket contributes a large reciprocal even when it occupies only part of a cell. Resampling the picture preserves that contribution.

Source: GfX §3.1 defines the two diagonal channels. §4.3 defines the volume average of the tensor. The RMS channel is an additional renderer diagnostic, stored alongside that average.

Two equal subvolumes: q = 0.25 and 0.75
⟨q⟩ = (0.25 + 0.75) / 2 = 0.50
⟨1/q⟩ = (4 + 1.333…) / 2 = 2.667
1/⟨q⟩ = 1 / 0.50 = 2.000

Average first and invert: the spatial weight loses 25%.
