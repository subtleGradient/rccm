Hyperslice / The surviving cuts

Four corners of the rendering space.

“Realistic or abstract?” is too weak a cut. A beautiful bubble can be an exact scalar instrument; a realistic vortex can hide the direction that matters. These two cuts tell us what learning work a view can do.

Razor 1: one sampled volume ↔ relations between volumes
Razor 2: recognizable shape ↔ readable measurement

We keep all four corners. The default view combines the instrument’s channel precision with enough physical shape to place the scene.

The generic voxel is a little instrument: a fixed sampling footprint, one capacity reading, a separate reciprocal spatial weight, a slip arrow, a handed twist mark and an unresolved-activity halo. Particle identity lives beside that state. A renderer can change its appearance without changing the sampled world.

Quads-PREP / Stress the picture

Make the rendering earn its meaning.

Perception: Does a sign reversal move the right marks? Compare the glyph with the matrix.

Stability: Do 1, 8 and 64 cells preserve the same volume averages? Opposing means must cancel without erasing RMS activity.

Impact: Can you predict the pressure push before seeing the arrow? Uniform pressure must leave equal opposed contributions visible.

The working model, stress tests and full slice map record the cuts and counterexamples.
