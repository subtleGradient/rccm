# 2026-09-28 — Tau Fluid Lab visual prototype

## User direction

Tom requested a React Three Fiber design toy that places a moving electron/positron tornadonut pair in a cube of tau fluid and lets him see the local asymmetric tensor evolve. He chose an existing pair, an authored field toy, direct core movement and rotation, gentle default travel, and four standalone views sharing visual components. He delegated the art direction. The selected look is a dark luminous fluid lab with blue and gold core accents.

## Visible mark

Built `examples/tau-fluid-lab/` with Flow, Cavities, Tensor and Playground pages. They share one field sampler, pair settings, timeline, 3D volume, tracer paths, slice, probe and matrix assembly. Pair settings survive page navigation through the URL. Browser checks covered desktop and 390-pixel mobile layouts, direct core dragging, page navigation and tensor visibility. Source tests cover matrix signs, pressure ledger, deterministic paths, void treatment, boundary flow and supported control extremes.

## Model boundary and learner map

The moving cores and their circulation are prescribed visual fixtures. Material paths integrate through the authored velocity; a normalized pressure allocation produces q, e and b for the focused GfX Cartesian matrix. The fixture does not generate charge or cavitation from the full RCCM equations. Charge identity remains independent of ordinary core rotation. No new user prediction was received, so this session supplies no evidence of new conceptual mastery.

The next learning probe in the toy is to pause Flow, tag one parcel just ahead of a travelling core, and predict whether it follows the core or bends around its cavity before replaying.

## Charge study revision

Tom found the first visual streamlines, twist marks and tau medium too subtle. His goal shifted to seeing why a tornadonut pair approaches or separates through the medium around the cavities and within their gap. He chose a guided close-up with the same scene flipped between opposite and like charge. The local TeX and charge-rotation audit support a pressure-mechanism illustration for an aligned pair, while leaving the general rotated core-to-field construction open.

Added a fifth React Three Fiber page, `charge.html`, with one cavity, gap, pressure, surface-push and release stages. The aligned toy sums two authored circulation fields, maps their speed to normalized static pressure, integrates that pressure over both complete torus surfaces, and moves both specimens with the resulting forces. Stable identity and charge sign are separate, allowing electron/electron comparison. Held stages show an explicit alignment fixture; release translates along its rail and stops before contact or cube exit. The same calculation supplies the tracer motion, pressure cut, gauges and surface-force readings. A cutaway comparison shows the ring and tube circulation routes; animated medium flecks and clearer streamline arrows carry the fluid motion visually.

The original Flow, Cavities, Tensor and Playground pages gained denser medium contrast, moving speckles with short trails, stronger directional streamlines, and larger labeled internal-twist axes. Model tests now include force sign, opposite reaction, deterministic release, finite pressure, voids, uniform-pressure cancellation and tangency at moving torus surfaces. Browser checks covered desktop and mobile charge layouts, both charge signs, release, and the existing Flow and Playground views. No new learner prediction was received, so this update does not close the charge derivation gap or establish conceptual mastery. A useful next probe: hold the pair at the surface-push stage, flip only B's charge, and predict which face's pressure arrows grow before release.
