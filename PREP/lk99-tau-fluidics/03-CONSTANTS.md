# LK-99 / Tau-Fluidics Controls

These are controls for this inquiry: constraints that must remain fixed while
candidate explanations are varied.

- **Scope control:** “LK-99 is not superconducting” does not entail “no
  room-temperature superconductor is possible.”
- **Observable control:** low or unmeasurable longitudinal DC resistance,
  magnetic flux exclusion/shielding, field suppression of the transition, and
  a reproducible thermodynamic/transport transition are distinct observables.
- **Levitation control:** partial motion or levitation over a permanent magnet
  is not unique to superconductivity.
- **Purity control:** a multiphase sample cannot assign an anomaly to one phase
  without phase-resolved evidence.
- **Transition control:** a first-order structural transition with thermal
  hysteresis is not interchangeable with a superconducting transition.
- **Geometry control:** a transverse/topological impedance is not automatically
  a longitudinal bulk resistivity.
- **Operator control:** \(\mu>0\) does not imply
  \(\mu\nabla^2\mathbf v\ne0\).
- **Dimension control:** \(De\) is dimensionless, but dimensional compatibility
  does not establish that its \(E\) equals a carrier’s \(k_BT\).
- **Model-layer control:** an electromechanical analogy does not by itself
  establish physical identity or empirical adequacy.
- **Falsification control:** a universal impossibility claim requires covering
  every admissible material/mechanism state, not merely one failed candidate.

An official measurement-oriented reference for the distinction between zero
DC resistance and magnetic flux behavior is NIST’s
[discussion of superconducting flux lattices](https://www.nist.gov/ncnr/flux-lattice-superconductors-and-melting).
