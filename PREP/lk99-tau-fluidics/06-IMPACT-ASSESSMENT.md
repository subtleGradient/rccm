# LK-99 / Tau-Fluidics Impact Assessment

## Practical audit

- A tau-fluidic impossibility claim cannot currently tell an experimentalist
  which composition to synthesize, what \(T_c\) to expect, or which
  transport/magnetization curve would falsify it.
- The useful immediate result is conventional: reject LK-99 as a
  room-temperature superconductor unless a new, phase-resolved sample
  independently reproduces the full transport and magnetic signature.
- A future RCCM superconductivity module should be benchmarked first against
  established low-\(T_c\), cuprate, and high-pressure hydride data before it is
  allowed to judge novel materials.

## Epistemic risks

- **Scope laundering:** turning “LK-99 failed” into “the phenomenon is
  impossible.”
- **Observable laundering:** turning quantum resistance, impedance, viscosity,
  or levitation into zero longitudinal resistance.
- **Symbol laundering:** inserting \(k_BT\) for an energy symbol because the
  units fit.
- **Analogy laundering:** treating fluid phase lock as evidence of a real
  condensate.
- **Retrofit risk:** choosing tau parameters after seeing each material’s
  transition.
- **Asymmetric skepticism:** demanding perfect evidence from conventional
  superconductivity while treating RCCM identities as established premises.

## Social audit

The viral replication burst had real scientific value: multiple groups rapidly
separated synthesis, impurity, magnetic, and transport explanations. The
failure mode was not public enthusiasm itself; it was allowing a vivid
levitation clip and transition-shaped graph to outrun phase-resolved,
independent evidence.

## Decision

- **LK-99:** reject the room-temperature-superconductor interpretation on the
  present evidence.
- **Room-temperature superconductivity in general:** open.
- **RCCM verdict:** unable to rule it out and unable to predict it.
- **Next honest RCCM move:** build and preregister a material-response model,
  then let prospective data attack it.
