# LK-99 / Tau-Fluidics Working Model

## Prior that was attacked

## Scope

“The viral room-temperature superconductor paper” is provisionally identified
as Lee, Kim, and Kwon’s July 2023 LK-99 preprint, *The First Room-Temperature
Ambient-Pressure Superconductor*.

Two claims must not be conflated:

1. **Specimen claim:** the reported LK-99 material superconducts above room
   temperature at ambient pressure.
2. **Possibility claim:** some material could superconduct above room
   temperature at ambient pressure.

> I expect the published LK-99 evidence to fail independent experimental
> replication. I do **not** expect the current RCCM tau-fluid corpus to prove
> that all room-temperature superconductivity is impossible, because it does
> not appear to contain a material-specific transport model, superconducting
> order parameter, thermal decoherence law, or derived upper bound on
> transition temperature.

## Assumptions to attack

- The viral paper is LK-99 rather than a different claim.
- Zero longitudinal DC resistance and a superconducting magnetic transition
  are the relevant target phenomena.
- RCCM’s transverse quantum impedance is not automatically the same observable
  as a sample’s four-probe longitudinal resistance.
- A positive tau-fluid viscosity does not automatically imply nonzero
  dissipation for every velocity field.
- Room temperature cannot be inserted as \(E=k_B T\) into an RCCM energy
  variable without first deriving that identification.

## Update

The prior survived, but its two halves have very different evidential status:

1. **LK-99:** independent phase-pure and LK-99-like samples are insulating,
   ferromagnetic/diamagnetic rather than superconducting, and do not show a
   superconducting transport-plus-magnetic transition. Cu₂S contamination
   reproduces the reported transition-shaped anomalies. The 2023 LK-99 claim is
   therefore strongly invalidated.
2. **Universal room-temperature possibility:** RCCM does not currently supply
   a no-go theorem. Its impedance, viscosity, and Deborah-number equations do
   not close over material composition, carrier dynamics, temperature, and a
   superconducting order parameter. Some RCCM limits explicitly permit zero
   kinematic resistance or make the viscous operator vanish.

## Working model

> The LK-99 claim should be rejected on independent empirical grounds. Current
> RCCM tau-fluidics neither validates nor invalidates the possibility of a
> different room-temperature, ambient-pressure superconductor. It presently
> supplies mechanical analogies and candidate variables, not a material
> transport theory with a derived \(T_c\).

The attempted tau-fluidic no-go theorem fails at the bridge from universal
continuum response to material-specific longitudinal DC transport. In
particular:

- \(Z_{\tau(\perp)}=h/q^2\) is identified with the von Klitzing resistance
  quantum, not derived as a bulk sample’s longitudinal resistivity.
- \(\mu>0\) does not force \(\mu\nabla^2\mathbf v\ne0\); uniform or harmonic
  flows can make the viscous term vanish.
- \(De=\alpha E/(m_ec^2)\) contains no derived identification
  \(E=k_BT\) for a solid’s charge carriers.
- the corpus itself describes a zero-kinematic-resistance circulation limit.

## What would change this model

The prior must be rewritten if any of the following is found:

- independent phase-pure LK-99 samples reproduce both zero resistance and a
  Meissner transition near or above 300 K;
- RCCM supplies a dimensionally closed constitutive route from material
  composition and temperature to electrical resistivity;
- RCCM derives a finite universal upper bound \(T_c<300\ \mathrm{K}\);
- RCCM’s own equations force positive dissipation for every admissible
  current-carrying state, including uniform and topologically protected flows.
