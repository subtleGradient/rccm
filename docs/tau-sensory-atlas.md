# A sensory atlas for the asymmetric tau tensor

You are floating in the battle room. Your body can adopt any posture. Gravity, electric response, magnetic response, contact and momentum each keep their own sensory voice. They can disagree about direction without being mixed into one generic feeling of “down.”[^status]

[^status]: This is a designed sensory code for imagination and interactive learning, inspired by the user's own examples. Sour/creamy tastes and augmented force sensations are invented readouts, not claims about ordinary physiology. RCCM supplies the proposed continuum interpretation; a sensor for a field, its derivative, or a material response must retain that distinction. The [paused-world guide](paused-world-pov.md) and [equation contract](openfoam-rccm/04-equation-contract-and-gaps.md) supply the prediction boundaries.

The user's starting ingredients are useful because they already separate location, texture, sign, magnitude and change: feet versus face; hair rising; sour versus creamy; gut-drop; clothes lagging; and a luminous mesh sampled with hands and mouth. Keep those ingredients stable, then vary them deliberately.

**One room, several directions, several kinds of sensation**

Give the body a frame: right hand is \(+x\), chest/front is \(+y\), and head is \(+z\). This is a right-handed frame. A posture change moves those sampling axes through the world. A single chosen “down” sets one axis; you still need a heading or roll convention to orient the other two.

Let gravity enter through a heavy belt around the pelvis. Let electric force enter through a distinct silk-like layer on the skin. Let magnetic translation tug through firmer seams of an imaginary vest. Let magnetic torque act through a shoulder–hip hoop. Let contact register only at the actual patches that touch the floor, string or support.

All those contributions can remain present together. A separate central cue reports the net acceleration after they combine. If electric force balances gravity, the belt and silk remain loaded while the central cue is quiet. If a table holds a magnetically attracted filing, vest tension and contact pressure remain while its center stays still.

These are addresses and textures for already computed contributions. The body location used to label a channel does not assert that gravity physically acts only on the pelvis or electricity only on skin.

**Three bodies to try on**

**The layered body:** keep one posture and give each contribution a distinct depth and texture. Gravity loads the deep belt, electricity loads the silk, magnetic translation loads the vest. This is the strongest starting candidate for hearing several forces at once without converting them into one sum.

**The constellation of ghost bodies:** keep the physical object in the middle, with several transparent sampling bodies overlaid. One places its feet toward gravitational down; another places its feet along the electric-force arrow for the selected probe; a third aligns its spine with the magnetic-field axis. Pin a shared landmark to show each body's roll. A zero force has no privileged down, so that ghost's alignment releases. These are coordinate choices; the magnetic-axis body is not being pulled toward its head merely because its spine follows the field.

**The membrane body:** spread your sensing skin over the whole object's boundary. Normal pressure feels like many little palms; tangential traction drags the skin sideways. Equal pushes on opposite sides can squeeze you while their translational effects cancel. Push the upper skin right and lower skin left: your center can remain still while a turning couple appears. This mode reveals why a single central arrow loses deformation and torque. It needs a surface traction field, geometry and the chosen stress-to-boundary law.

**Keep the user's yellow–blue landscape, with two explicit sampling modes**

In **charge mode**, a mouthful of material tastes sour or creamy according to its signed charge at the declared sampling scale. Saturated yellow/blue marks sit on the charged material or sources. A neutral object can still contain separated positive and negative patches when you zoom in; a coarse net-charge sample can cancel them.

In **electrostatic potential mode**, a translucent yellow–blue landscape represents voltage relative to a chosen reference. Taste *differences* between two places. The electric field points down that potential landscape, \(\mathbf E=-\nabla\phi\). The sign of the local voltage is not the sign of charge physically present there. [Field from potential](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-2/pages/7-4-determining-field-from-potential), [potential reference](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-2/pages/7-2-electric-potential-and-potential-difference)

Test the distinction beside a positively charged sphere. The sphere itself tastes sour in charge mode. A charge-free point outside it can have no charge flavour while your electric-field brush is strong. In potential mode, that same outside point can lie on a positive voltage landscape relative to infinity. Electric flux through a closed surface tracks enclosed charge; local field strength is a different reading. [Gauss's law](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-2/pages/6-2-explaining-gausss-law)

Creamy is a contrasting flavour label, not a chemical inverse of sour. Hair height is a chosen magnitude meter. Neutral taste means zero under the selected charge measurement, not “nothing electrical is happening.”

**Where these senses attach to the asymmetric tensor**

Use the focused source's local Cartesian form first. Define

\[
q=\alpha_s^2,\qquad
\mathbf e=\alpha\mathbf v_\perp/c,\qquad
\mathbf b=\alpha t_p\boldsymbol\Omega.
\]

Then

\[
\hat U=
\begin{pmatrix}
-q&-e_x&-e_y&-e_z\\
e_x&q^{-1}&-b_z&b_y\\
e_y&b_z&q^{-1}&-b_x\\
e_z&-b_y&b_x&q^{-1}
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \text{basis }(ct,x,y,z).
\]

One pressure-capacity value and two oriented three-component fields populate this matrix family. The three spatial diagonal weights are linked copies of \(1/q\). They do not independently permit the suit to become hard in one direction and soft in another. A more general symmetric tensor or material-response tensor can carry that extra information.

Source: [GfX Section 3](</Users/tom/Library/Mobile Documents/com~apple~CloudDocs/RCCM/RCCM-GfX-2.tex:88>) and [seven numbers, sixteen slots](../rccm-data.md#seven-numbers-sixteen-slots). The normalized \(\mathbf e,\mathbf b\) are dimensionless; measured volts/metre and tesla need their stated calibration. A general tensor or changed moving observer need not retain this seven-coefficient form.

Read the matrix as three sensory families:

- **Capacity and diagonal structure:** the suit's available room and linked spatial weights. The negative sign on \(U_{00}=-q\) belongs to the temporal convention. It must never trigger the creamy “negative electric charge” taste.
- **Time–space electric-like slots:** the directional slip brush. Each \(e_i\) appears twice with opposite signs, \(U_{0i}=-e_i\) and \(U_{i0}=e_i\). Those signs are ordered tensor components, not two separate charge species.
- **Space–space magnetic-like slots:** oriented turning planes. A \(b_z\) channel pairs the right–front plane; \(b_x\) pairs front–head; \(b_y\) pairs head–right. The oriented axle identifies which plane is turning and with what handedness.

For the spatial antisymmetric block, its algebraic action on a direction \(\mathbf n\) is \(\mathbf b\times\mathbf n\). Let \(\mathbf b\) point toward your head. A rightward test direction maps forward; a forward test direction maps left; a headward test direction maps to zero. Reverse \(\mathbf b\) and the sideways mappings reverse.

This can be played as a sleeve nudging a probing arm sideways. It is an algebraic tensor probe, not a prediction that the magnetic field exerts that force on an ordinary arm. The force version additionally needs the arm's charge, motion and magnetic response. For example, the charged-particle magnetic law uses \(\mathbf v\times\mathbf B\), the opposite order from \(\mathbf B\times\mathbf v\).

**Feel symmetry by swapping two probes**

Take two four-component algebraic probes \(\mathbf a,\mathbf b\) in the displayed spacetime basis. For the symmetric part \(S\), their reading satisfies
\(\mathbf a^T S\mathbf b=\mathbf b^T S\mathbf a\).
For the antisymmetric part \(A\), the swapped reading changes sign:
\(\mathbf a^T A\mathbf b=-\mathbf b^T A\mathbf a\).

Encode the reading as a signed tug between two labelled cuffs. Swapping the probe labels preserves the symmetric cue and reverses the antisymmetric cue. Two purely spatial probes sample the spatial block; the electric-like time–space slots require a temporal component in a probe. This is a comparison of tensor arguments; it does not assert that physically walking east then north has the same meaning.

A single same-direction squeeze cannot reveal the antisymmetric part because \(\mathbf n^T A\mathbf n=0\). That is why the sensory vocabulary needs an oriented plane/axle probe in addition to stretch and pressure. Algebraic verification requires no assumption that the matrix is already a complete material force law.

**Four chords to inhabit**

**Balanced charged bead.** You are sour-positive. Gravity loads the pelvic belt toward your feet. The electric silk draws toward your crown. At exact balance the central acceleration cue is silent, while both source channels stay strong. Change your charge to creamy-negative without changing the imposed field: the silk reverses, joining the belt. Roll sideways: the same world directions enter through different body surfaces.

**Orbiting rock.** The gravity belt points toward the planet through your ribs. The velocity ghost leads along the tangent. Your ordinary support layer is absent. There is no outward source to invent for inertia: the existing movement is already present in the ghost. A supported acceleration would make face, shirt or kilt lag and load against their attachments; those relative motions require an attached-body/clothing model. A sustained gut-drop can be chosen as an augmented free-fall marker, but should not be mistaken for a direct measurement of the gravitational field.

**Iron filing.** A magnetic axle passes through your body. Its strength changes as your imaginary hands reach nearer the pole. Tiny internal magnetic orientations reorganize; the vest's translational tug and shoulder hoop's torque remain separate. The table presses at specific contact patches. A polarity reversal can reverse the sampled axle and induced magnetization while the attractive vest tug survives. A permanently magnetized or clamped object has a different response.

**Storm balloon.** Charge flavour and hair height identify your own charge state. The electric brush samples the imposed field; the silk's net tug reports your coupled mechanical response. Buoyancy, air loading and weight have their own textures. Outside you, a separate fizz marks regions where a specified gas-discharge model predicts favorable electron growth. It can develop while your resultant motion cue remains quiet. The fizz records air's changing conducting state, not the tau tensor reaching its absolute yield limit.

**A vortex and a cavity are two additional experiments.** Become a little marked sphere in a sheared flow: its internal rotation cue samples local curl, while its skin can also distort. Then become a closed ribbon encircling the core: a signed travelling touch reports circulation around that chosen loop. The ribbon can register circulation even when a small exterior sphere reports zero local curl. If the model also contains a cavity, put its boundary against the membrane body and display the missing interior as a visible region. Boundary motion, recoverable stretching and circulating flow get separate senses. Neither circulation nor low local capacity alone supplies a complete law for forming or collapsing the cavity.

**Posture changes need two modes**

In **sampler mode**, your imaginary body is a noninvasive set of axes and sensors. Standing on your hands or lying face up changes where world-directed cues land on the body. For a proper spatial rotation \(R\) taking body axes into world axes, vectors in body coordinates are \(R^T\mathbf e\) and \(R^T\mathbf b\). Your scalar charge flavour and \(q\) remain unchanged. Set \(T=\operatorname{diag}(1,R)\); the covariant tensor reads \(\hat U_{body}=T^T\hat U_{world}T\). There is no physical force change just because you chose a different camera posture.

In **object mode**, rotating the actual filing, dipole, sail or elongated cavity changes its physical relation to the field and may change the force/torque. Its charge sign still does not change merely through rotation. A change in observer velocity is a third operation, requiring a full frame transformation; electric and magnetic splits are observer-dependent.

Keep the distinction visible: **turn my sensing frame** and **turn the physical object** are different controls.

**What one tensor sample cannot taste**

The local \(q,\mathbf e,\mathbf b\) family supports its capacity, slip and twist readouts. It does not by itself supply:

- the pressure slope that produces the reduced gravity arrow;
- the separation of background and local loads inside \(q=\alpha_g^2\alpha_a^2\);
- charge density, voltage reference, or current from one field value;
- velocity, angular momentum, body charge, magnetization and material history;
- forces constrained by contacts, or extended circulation/topology;
- a unique allocation of nonlinear interactions to named sources.

The [data guide's hidden-load example](../rccm-data.md#what-the-matrix-leaves-undetermined) demonstrates why equal \(q\) values can conceal different ambient/local splits. The [weak-static gravity law](</Users/tom/Library/Mobile Documents/com~apple~CloudDocs/RCCM/RCCM-GfX-2.tex:387>) explicitly requires a derivative. Source-separated sensations therefore require source labels and the state needed to evaluate the chosen force decomposition. Muting playback preserves that state; removing a source requires recomputing its consequences.
