# The fluid does all the doing

**A planet is a frothy storm in the universe fluid. An electron is a persistent pattern in that fluid. The fluid carries every squeeze, slip, twist and transfer of momentum.**[^premise]

[^premise]: This guide takes the universal fluid and particles as stable cavity–flow patterns as the project's axioms. It develops that picture using `RCCM-GfX-2.tex`; the mathematical notes distinguish its stated equations from the further fluid solutions they call for.

## Stand inside the fluid

Fill your entire imagined world with universe fluid. It extends around your
back, over your head, through the distance and beyond everything you can see.
Give yourself a viewpoint inside one small patch, with a second awareness
that can wander through the whole surrounding volume.

Let pressure feel like squeeze across the patch's boundary. Let transverse
slip feel like silk moving across it. These cues read the fluid's pressure
and relative sideways motion. Keep them separate while feeling both at once.

Now let time run. Every patch already has motion. Neighbouring patches press,
slide and turn against one another. Their changing arrangement carries
momentum through the volume. A disturbance reaches the next neighbourhood
through the fluid's evolution.

Some of that motion organizes into a persistent circulation. Follow it all
the way around, through its depth and around its winding. The moving fluid
maintains an opening within itself. The shape of that opening belongs to the
balance of motion and stress surrounding it.

There is your tornadonut.

Keep watching the fluid. It is maintaining the cavity, shaping it, carrying
its surrounding disturbance and changing where the whole pattern sits.

## The particle name belongs to the persistent pattern

What we call an **electron** is a recurring stable organization of universe
fluid that maintains a cavity and carries the blue, negative polarity of the
electric slip pattern.

What we call a **proton** is another recurring stable organization of the same
fluid, maintaining its own cavity structure and carrying the yellow,
positive polarity. Polarity is one property of these patterns; their complete
structures also carry their other differences.

The cavity's outline gives your eye somewhere to recognize the pattern. Its
stability lives in the moving fluid: circulation, twist, pressure and the way
those motions sustain one another through time.

Imagine marking one tiny fluid parcel white. Follow the white parcel, then
follow the enduring pattern. Those are two different things to track. The
parcel follows its material path. The persistent pattern has a location,
shape and history maintained by the fluid's evolving organization.

Now make the cavity silhouettes transparent. Leave the surrounding fluid
visible. You can still follow where the motion gathers, where pressure is
depleted, where slip extends and where neighbouring motion changes. That is
the activity we are trying to understand.

## A planet is a storm with a pressure footprint

Pull your awareness outward until an enormous population of maintained
cavities fits inside your view. The fluid winds around them, through the
spaces between them and throughout the larger structure they compose.

This frothing, circulating region is the planet.

Its organized motion occupies part of the fluid's pressure budget. Around
the region, the remaining static pressure forms a continuous terrain:
lower toward the loaded region, higher farther into the surrounding soup.
The storm's pressure footprint extends through fluid beyond the crowded
part we identify as the planet.

Place another small, persistent fluid pattern in that terrain. Feel the
pressure over its entire surrounding boundary. The higher-pressure side
presses more strongly. The fluid's momentum balance changes the motion of
the pattern toward the lower-pressure region.

That is the pressure-shove picture of falling.

“Down” is whichever direction leads down the local pressure terrain. Turn
your head, roll the planet in your imagination, view the scene from beneath
it: that physical relationship remains. The fluid has no preferred page-up.

Keep both regions free to move. The smaller pattern and the planetary storm
belong to one evolving fluid configuration. Keep their existing motion too:
a sideways-moving pattern can have its path bent by the pressure terrain.

The pressure budget is a ledger of the current state. Persistent motion can
occupy that budget without consuming a fresh, irrecoverable allowance every
instant. Energy moves among motion, stored response and neighbouring regions
as the fluid evolves.

## Two patterns share one fluid

Bring a yellow pattern and a blue pattern into the same neighbourhood. Allow
each to have any orientation and whatever motion it already has.

Their surrounding disturbances meet in a volume. That volume wraps around
the patterns, extends behind them, passes above and below them, and continues
into the rest of the universe fluid. At every place and moment there is one
actual fluid state to evolve.

The shared flow changes where motion is concentrated, where static pressure
remains and how directional stresses are distributed. The fluid maintaining
each cavity is part of this same changing arrangement. As momentum is
exchanged through the medium, the locations and motions of the maintained
patterns change.

For opposite electrical polarities, the electric part of this rearrangement
has an approaching tendency. For like polarities, it has a separating
tendency. “Attraction” and “repulsion” name those net tendencies of the
maintained patterns. The pressure, slip and momentum transfer are distributed
through the fluid that produces them.

The useful piece of your seesaw intuition is here: **the relationship between
two flow contributions can reinforce motion or cancel part of it.** That
changes the pressure left in the shared fluid. Reinforcement in one region
and cancellation in another belong to the same full configuration.

To find the resulting shove, gather the contributions over a complete
surrounding surface. A narrow view through the middle samples only part of
the exchange. The full surrounding fluid determines the net transfer.

Now scramble the orientations in your imagination. Preserve each pattern's
polarity and follow its whole surrounding flow as it changes. The story still
has the same ingredients: one fluid, maintained structures, distributed
stress and momentum transferred through their shared neighbourhood.

Reorienting one real pattern can change detailed nearby flow and produce
turning or other orientation-dependent effects. Its polarity survives that
rotation. An explanation of the net charge interaction has to carry both
facts together.

## Put the mathematics back inside the fluid

At a fluid event \((\mathbf x,t)\), GfX's remaining-capacity reading is

\[
q(\mathbf x,t)
=\frac{P_{\mathrm{static}}(\mathbf x,t)}{P_c},
\qquad
P_c=\tfrac12\rho_\tau c^2.
\]

Here \(q\) is dimensionless; \(P\) is pressure; \(\rho_\tau\) is the
reference fluid density; and \(c\) is the acoustic phase limit. The dynamic
load in GfX §2 is

\[
P_{\mathrm{dyn}}
=\tfrac12\rho_\tau
\left(v_\parallel^2+v_\perp^2+v_\circlearrowleft^2\right).
\]

The three terms account for the stated orthogonal translation/compression,
transverse and rotational channels. Together with the other counted loads,
they occupy the budget that leaves \(P_{\mathrm{static}}\). Sample these
quantities in the fluid where the state is defined.

For the weak, static pressure terrain and slow moving pattern used in GfX
§5.3, the gravity reading is

\[
\mathbf a_g
\simeq-\frac{1}{\rho_\tau}\nabla P_{\mathrm{static}}
=-\frac{c^2}{2}\nabla q.
\]

The gradient reads the pressure change in all three spatial directions at
once. Multiplying it by the constants gives an acceleration vector. Rotate
the scene and that vector rotates with the terrain.

For the electric-slip reading, GfX §3 writes

\[
\mathbf e=\alpha\frac{\mathbf v_\perp}{c},
\qquad
\hat U_{0i}=-e_i,
\qquad
\hat U_{i0}=e_i.
\]

The dimensionless vector \(\mathbf e\) records local slip; \(\alpha\) is
the transverse coupling ratio. Its individual component signs depend on
the chosen axes. The electrical polarity of the maintained pattern is a
property that the complete fluid construction must preserve through rotations.

Where it is appropriate to represent the shared slip by two added
contributions, the pressure calculation is

\[
\begin{aligned}
P_{\mathrm{dyn},\perp}
&=\tfrac12\rho_\tau|\mathbf v_1+\mathbf v_2|^2\\
&=\tfrac12\rho_\tau|\mathbf v_1|^2
 +\tfrac12\rho_\tau|\mathbf v_2|^2
 +\rho_\tau\mathbf v_1\cdot\mathbf v_2.
\end{aligned}
\]

The last term reads the relationship between the two local flows. Holding
the remaining loads fixed, its corresponding static-pressure change is

\[
\Delta P_{\mathrm{static,int}}
=-\rho_\tau\mathbf v_1\cdot\mathbf v_2.
\]

This is a pressure change relative to the separate self-loads. The total
dynamic pressure remains the nonnegative squared length of the combined
velocity. The dot product uses the full three-dimensional directions; it
has the same value after a common rotation of those directions. Constructing
the actual interacting flows is a dynamical job beyond this algebraic identity.

### Momentum crosses a complete boundary

Imagine a closed surface entirely surrounded by fluid. Its shape and
orientation are yours to choose. Mechanical stress \(\boldsymbol\sigma\)
gives the traction across each little surface patch:

\[
\mathbf t(\mathbf n)=\boldsymbol\sigma\mathbf n,
\qquad
\boldsymbol\sigma=-P_{\mathrm{static}}\mathbf I+\boldsymbol\tau,
\]

where \(\mathbf n\) is the outward unit normal and \(\boldsymbol\tau\)
collects the remaining directional mechanical stress. The net stress force is

\[
\mathbf F_{\mathrm{stress}}
=\oint_{\partial V}\boldsymbol\sigma\mathbf n\,dA.
\]

Equal isotropic pressure cancels in this integral. Directional and spatially
varying stresses contribute according to their full distribution. Fluid
crossing a fixed surface also carries momentum with it; that transported
momentum belongs in the complete balance.

GfX expresses its spacetime conservation statement as

\[
\nabla_\mu\hat T^{\mu\nu}=0,
\qquad
\hat T_{\mu\nu}=P_c(\hat U_{\mu\nu}-\eta_{\mu\nu})
\]

in its linear stress map. This ties changes and transport to the surrounding
state. The ordinary traction notation above and GfX's four-dimensional
stress/momentum-flux notation require their respective sign and transport
conventions when translating between them.

### Carry the whole construction through a rotation

Let \(R\) be any ordinary three-dimensional rotation. Transform the fluid,
the surface and the directions together:

\[
\mathbf v'=R\mathbf v,
\qquad \mathbf n'=R\mathbf n,
\qquad
\boldsymbol\sigma'=R\boldsymbol\sigma R^{\mathsf T}.
\]

Then

\[
\mathbf v'_1\cdot\mathbf v'_2
=\mathbf v_1\cdot\mathbf v_2,
\qquad
\mathbf F'_{\mathrm{stress}}=R\mathbf F_{\mathrm{stress}}.
\]

The pressure accounting is unchanged and the shove rotates with the world.
Independently turning one physical pattern inside its neighbours creates a
changed fluid configuration to evolve. It retains its charge identity while
its detailed stresses can change. Following a moving observer also requires
the full spacetime transformation of the tensor and fields, beyond an
ordinary spatial rotation.

### Work is the fluid changing its stored arrangement

Pressure has units \(\mathrm{Pa}=\mathrm{J/m^3}\). Stress transfers
momentum, and motion under that stress can transfer energy. For the
translation of a tracked pattern,

\[
\delta W=\mathbf F\cdot\delta\mathbf x.
\]

Your available-work intuition belongs to this exchange. The entire
configuration supplies the storage, the stress and the permitted motion.
The local pressure budget tells us how the fluid is loaded; its spatial
organization tells us where momentum is transferred. A force can bend
existing motion without transferring energy at that instant when force and
velocity are perpendicular.
