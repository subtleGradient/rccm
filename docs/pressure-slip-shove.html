01 / Pressure shove

The harder squeeze wins.

The circulating and moving medium occupies part of the pressure budget. Less static pressure remains near the mass. At the pictured instant, the fluid above your cavity presses harder than the fluid below. The net shove points down.

↓ 8.99 m/s² downward

Numerical example: upward is +z; ∂q/∂z = 2 × 10⁻¹⁶ m⁻¹, in a weak, nearly full-capacity region. This is acceleration, not velocity.

q = Pstatic / Pcag ≈ −(c²/2) ∇qFg ≈ −Veff ∇Pstatic

GfX §5.3: static, weak field; slow test body; constant Pc = ½ρτc². Here Veff = mτ/ρτ.

Your gravity picture fits this route. “Used up” means stored as kinematic load, not energy destroyed. The model’s remaining capacity q is a scalar: 0 < q ≤ 1 on the regular branch. At q = 0 the spatial weight 1/q becomes singular.

02 / Slip shove

The overlap changes the squeeze.

Place two charged tornadonuts beside each other. In the TeX’s facing-flow picture, opposite charges reinforce the slip in the gap. Faster combined flow consumes more of the budget there. Static pressure drops, so the surrounding fluid drives the pair together.

→ ← Reinforcing flow · attraction

Reverse only the right charge to compare the proposed like-charge case: facing flows cancel and static pressure recovers in the gap.

ei = α v⊥,i / cÛ0i = −ei   ·   Ûi0 = +eiPdyn,⊥ = ½ρτ|v₁ + v₂|²

GfX §§2–3 gives the ledger and matrix entries; §16.2 sketches Coulomb attraction. Condensed’s “Coulomb’s Law” gives the pair cross-term and repulsion account.

The signed seesaw belongs to the overlap. ei is a signed, dimensionless velocity component. Charge Q labels the defect’s polarity. Neither is a −50% to +50% static-pressure meter. A negative component can simply mean “toward −x.”

Keep the magnitudes / Turn one arrow

A signed cross-term inside a positive budget.

At one point in the gap, add the two velocity arrows first. Then square the length of their sum. Each flow contributes a positive self-term. Their dot product contributes a term that can be positive, zero, or negative.

½ρτ|v₁ + v₂|²= ½ρτ|v₁|² + ½ρτ|v₂|² + ρτv₁ · v₂

Holding the other loads fixed, the corresponding change in static pressure is ΔPstatic,int = −ρτv₁ · v₂. A negative change means less pressure than the sum-of-self-terms reference. It does not make the total pressure negative.

The boundary matters

Equal squeeze cancels. A uniform electric field can still shove.

Add the pushes over the whole skin.

For a closed boundary, uniform isotropic pressure gives zero net translation. An imposed spatially constant mechanical stress also has zero net surface force. Internal loading or torque needs its own check.

Fpressure = −∮∂V P n dA
= −∫V ∇P dV

n is the outward unit normal. Opposite surface normals make equal pushes cancel. This is the continuum surface-force identity; using the defect’s Veff adds GfX’s mass/displacement identification.

Keep the field. Change the receiver.

A positive test charge follows the applied E arrow; a negative charge goes against it. The field is already a direction for force per charge. It can stay constant while electric potential changes across the room.

→ Same E · positive charge · force right

Fe = Q E   ·   E = −∇φ

Conventional electrostatic comparison, in SI units. The neutral option is an ideal test object with no induced response. The external field omits the object’s own field; the full interacting stress around a charge need not be uniform. Feynman I–14.

So narrow the “same tensor all around” rule. Zero net shove requires the relevant surface pushes or force contributions to cancel. Equal applied-field readings alone do not guarantee that. The charged object and its coupling belong in the calculation.

Your common thread

Available work belongs to a change of configuration.

Move the chosen object a little. If the configuration’s potential energy drops, that energy can become motion. In a conservative, quasistatic description, the force points down the object’s potential-energy slope:

F = −∇Epot   ·   δW = F · δx = −δEpot

Gravity: one slope for both charge signs.

With mτ, ρτ and the background fixed, GfX §5.3 gives the pressure route. Integrating its force gives:

Epot,g = VeffPstatic + constant

This is a derived effective potential for the stated small-body, weak-field law. Changing yellow to blue while preserving mass does not reverse its gravitational force.

Electricity: the charge signs change the slope.

In the standard point-charge comparison, the interaction energy and force on body 2 are:

Epot,e(r) = ke Q₁Q₂ / r
F₂ = (ke Q₁Q₂ / r²) r̂

r̂ points from body 1 toward body 2; ke = 1/(4πε₀). Like signs lower energy by separating; opposite signs lower it by approaching. Both free bodies respond. These are conventional comparison laws, not a derivation from the GfX matrix. Feynman I–12, I–14.

Pressure has units of energy per volume. That makes pressure and work closely connected, but a local q reading alone is not a quantity of extractable work. Specify the object, its displacement, its coupling and the allowed process. For electric response, even the word “higher potential” needs the charge sign: Epot = Qφ.

The stronger electric force also needs its coupling and scale. A signed axis versus an unsigned axis does not determine their strength ratio.

Three separate steps

Attraction is a start. Orbitals require more.

A yellow knot surrounded by blue knots has many interacting fields. In the linear electrostatic comparison, potentials and field vectors add; blue–blue repulsion also contributes. In the fluid ledger, velocities add before the square. “More blue overlap” does not by itself specify a stable motion or an allowed atomic state.

02 / Bounded motion

What prevents collision or escape?

Existing velocity, angular momentum, energy transfer and boundary dynamics decide the path. An inward force can bend sideways motion; attraction alone does not supply that motion.

GfX §16.2 proposes advection–vorticity coupling and a sideways deflection for positronium. The closed evolution and stability calculation remain to be supplied.

03 / Quantized states

Which patterns are allowed?

GfX adds a standing-wave and circulation constraint. It proposes discrete radii and binding energies:

∮ v⊥ · dl = n h/me
rn ∝ n²   ·   |En| ∝ n⁻²

These appear in §16’s future-work roadmap. A field/boundary solution, stable modes and their observed distribution are further obligations.

An atomic orbital denotes a quantum state with a spatial amplitude and probability distribution. A curved track or a dense residence cloud is a different output. Connecting a fluid trajectory model to measured orbital patterns needs the corresponding state and measurement rule. Feynman III–19: hydrogen states.

Read the stress bridge, units, and remaining construction

Pressure and shear meet at a boundary.

In ordinary continuum notation, let σ be the mechanical traction stress and n the outward surface normal. Splitting σ = −P I + σshear gives:

F = ∮∂V σn dA
= ∫V [−∇P + ∇·σshear] dV

This is the precise sense in which both normal squeeze and tangential traction can add up to a shove. A shear tensor supplies directional tractions, not a second scalar pressure. Net torque and spin need an additional angular-momentum balance.

GfX §4 defines a four-dimensional stress deviation T̂μν = Pc(Ûμν − ημν). Its §5.1 compatible-flow balance includes ½ factors and ρdyn; §5.2 uses a different defect inertia; §5.3 gives the restricted gravity law used here. The time–space electric entries are not, just by their position, a three-dimensional tangential surface traction. Translating these into σ and a force on a charged boundary needs the stated conventions and constitutive/source rules.
