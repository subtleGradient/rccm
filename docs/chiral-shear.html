In-model illustration: the metal shapes are handles for seeing flow. In RCCM, a persistent “particle” would be a cavity maintained by the fluid itself.

03 · One signed geometry

The pattern sets its own axis.

Call tipward distance z, thread pitch p (positive), and the winding sign h. A right-hand thread has h = +1; its mirror has h = −1. The smooth case has no thread sign.

thread ridge: θ(z) = h · 2πz / pconstrained advance: vz = h · pω / 2π

Here positive angular speed ω means clockwise viewed from the head toward the tip. Thus right-hand clockwise and left-hand counterclockwise both give positive tipward travel.

04 · Orientation survives

Turning the whole scene changes the view.

Rotate the driver, passage, fluid, and observer together. Their local relationships stay intact. A reflected winding changes the pattern itself, so the fluid’s chiral shear reverses.

The metal props illustrate a geometric possibility. The page does not calculate charge from a cavity’s fluid equations. The RCCM text proposes that charge polarity tracks the winding of a stable fluid-supported cavity; the screw analogy makes that proposal spatially legible.

One useful next move: pick the blue passage and tumble it until it looks yellow from a particular angle. Check whether any actual thread crossing has reversed.
